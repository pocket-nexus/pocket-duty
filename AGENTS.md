# Repository instructions

- Pocket Duty owns the export page (`web/src/pocket`, `web/export.html`), the compiler and the pack (`crates/duty-cook`, `crates/duty-pack`), the match (`crates/duty-sim`), the interface (`ui/`, `crates/duty-interface`), the PSP program (`psp/`), the Nintendo 3DS program (`n3ds/`) and the browser's (`wgpu/`). PocketJS and Pocket3D are the submodule `vendor/pocketjs`, which owns the browser kernel and the Pocket3D player (`devices/web/pocket-web-wgpu`, `tools/pocket3d-web.ts`).
- Do not edit the submodule to fix application behavior. Send reusable changes to PocketJS, then update the pinned revision here.
- `web/` outside `web/src/pocket` and `web/export.html` is the reference, Claude of Duty by mshumer (MIT, `web/LICENSE`), unmodified. Do not edit it: the export page imports it and records what it produces.
- **This repository is public.** DutyIR, packs, builds, captures and the listing's pictures stay out of Git, under the ignored `.pocket-build/` and `dist/`. Do not commit a build or a pack, and do not attach one to a GitHub release: the packages are built by `bun tools/release.ts` from `main` and uploaded to Pocket Studio (`--upload`), where the game's page is.
- The listing on Pocket Studio (`bun tools/listing.ts`, README "The listing") is `listing/listing.json`, the words and the takes, in Git, and the clips and stills the tool films from a PSP. A picture is a take of a run: control words in `boot.txt`, filmed with `film=<frames>` from the program's first frame. A take's frame numbers hold for one build: after a change to the match or the renderer, film the runs again and look at them before uploading. A picture the emulator drew is never uploaded as the console's.
- Use Conventional Commits for commits and pull requests (`type(scope): summary`).
- The look of a surface is defined once, by the reference's materials. The export page runs them and records what they produce (colour before light, light without the sun, the tone table, the sky); the compiler lowers that. Do not re-implement a material in the compiler.
- A change to the export, the compiler or the profile goes through `bun tools/duty.ts export` and `cook`; the pack and its receipt are outputs under `.pocket-build/`. A device difference is a value of a profile (`profiles/psp30.json`, `profiles/n3ds30.json`, `profiles/vita60.json`) or a lowering in `crates/duty-cook`, not a second copy of the world.
- The pack's records are `crates/duty-pack`; the compiler and the device read them from there. A record that changes size needs a new cook: the device reads a section as whole records and stops with `scene section` when the sizes disagree.
- `duty-sim` and `duty-pack` build without `std` (`default-features = false`): `core`, `alloc` and `libm` only. What moves (the player, the soldiers, the weapons, the effects, the mixer, the choice of what a frame draws) lives in `duty-sim` with a host test, and the device only draws it. The posing of soldiers and of the held weapon (`duty_sim::pose`) and the choice of sounds (`duty_sim::hear`) are there too: `psp/`, `n3ds/core` and `vita/` call the same functions.
- **The held weapon is carried by turns about the eye.** What brings it to the sights and what lowers it in a sprint are a turn and a place for each weapon, written by the export (`carried` in `web/src/pocket/figures.js`: the pose's matrix times the inverse of the hip's) and kept in the pack (`Info::ads_*`, `Info::sprint_*`). The differences of two poses' turns and places are not that carry: they swing the weapon about the eye and out of the picture. Two functions pose it and both read the pack: `duty_sim::pose::Posed::held` (PSP and 3DS) and `Figures::held` in `wgpu/src/figures.rs` (the browser). A change to one is a change to the other, and a walk at the stick's full travel is a sprint: look at it. **A profile says how the arms are taken from the export (`figures.arms`, `Arms` in `crates/duty-cook/src/figures.rs`)**: `"skin"` draws each part again as one closed surface (the handhelds: a hand's 45 shells simplified apart are slivers and a gap at the wrist), `"open"` keeps the shells and draws the open ones from both sides (the PS Vita), no key keeps the export's shells as they are. `duty-cook figures` draws `<weapon>_hands.png` at the profile's screen size: look at it after any change to a held weapon's budget or mesh.
- The match steps **60 times a second** from one seeded generator (`duty_sim::rng`), two steps a frame. A rule taken from the reference names its file and lines in a comment. A rule this game adds says so: the match itself (10 minutes, first to 30, reinforcements, respawn), the squads' search for a player they have lost, the difficulty scale.
- **A map is `maps/<id>/place.js`**: floors drawn by hand as polygons at their heights, in the place's own coordinates (metres east and north of its middle, heights above its lowest floor), beside its `map.json`, `look.js` and `views.json` (`maps/README.md`). **No file lists the maps: a map's change touches only `maps/<id>/`**. `web/src/pocket/placekit.js` builds it from the reference's kit with no street, gate or perimeter: walls where a floor's edge has nothing beyond it, faces where a lower floor lies beyond, caps over the blocks between. `placelook.js` adds the map's own surfaces to the reference's library and palette. The first map is the reference's own street. `bun tools/duty.ts export|walk|cook|look --map <id>`; `tools/maps.ts` says where each map's files lie under `.pocket-build/`. No device and no crate has code for a particular map.
- **Nothing of another game's files enters this repository, a pack, a package or a page, and nothing is converted**: no vertex, texture, texel, sound, entity list, file name or map name, no name of that game or its series, and no marking from its walls. A plan is typed by hand against its author's own study pictures of a place from above; the study and its likeness script stay outside every repository, and no committed file names where they are. No tool here reads another game's map file.
- A place's floors lie at any height: the export's `scene.json` names their surfaces (`ground`), and the cook takes the ground's shadow picture at each floor's own height (`Lighting::floors`). In the walk grid a step is 0.45 m at most and a slope 0.5 m a metre at most; ground under a roof or a lintel is found by `mapnav.js`. `bun tools/duty.ts walk --map <id>` shows the grid from the export, before a cook.
- **A device holds one map's pack.** The browser reads another in place of it (`App::leave`, `Duty::map_wanted`). The PSP reads the other map's pack in the running program too (`Held::read` in `psp/src/main.rs`): a map's sections lie in two stretches `psp/src/mem.rs` cuts once for the largest map there is a pack for, the kernel's remainder and a stretch of the arena's tail, and the next map's sections take them over (`release_map`); the match and the renderer's lists are dropped first, the sound thread lets go of its sections (`audio::rest`), and the guest's collector runs. Read a section only through `PackFile::resident` and add its tag to `store::RESIDENT`, or the stretch is cut too short. A run that had room only for a smaller map than the one asked for still writes `map.txt` and starts itself again with `sceKernelLoadExec` (`store::start_again`). **`changes=N` in boot.txt is a scripted run** (`bun tools/psp.ts emu --ctl "changes=20 held=4096"`, or `run --boot` on a console): the program changes its map N times with no hand on it and writes a line a change to `changes.txt` where the packs are, with the arena's free bytes, its largest free block, the frames after the change and the opens made off the main thread. `held=<KiB>` keeps memory from the program before its arena is sized, so a PSPLINK start or the emulator runs in an XMB start's memory. **Every open by a path without a device is made on the thread the program started on** (`store::open` counts one made elsewhere as a stray open; the count must stay 0: from the XMB such an open fails, and PSPLINK and the emulator let it through). Twenty changes in PPSSPPHeadless, with 4 MiB held back, leave the arena's free bytes and its largest block at the same values from the third change; not checked on a console. A 3DS frees the pack's sections and its textures and reads the other map's in the running program (`dt_wanted`, `dt_drop` and `load` in `n3ds/src/main.c`): checked in Azahar, not on a console. A PS Vita gives the geometry's memory and the pictures' video memory back and reads the other map's in the running program (`World::leave`, `World::read` in `vita/src/main.rs`): checked on a console. **Every device reads a pack through `pocket.packs`** (PocketJS's `pocket3d-packs`: pieces named by their bytes; `tools/packs.ts` cuts and brings them): the browser from `pack/<hash>.packs#<map>`, the PSP beside the EBOOT or on `host0:/duty/`, the 3DS from `sdmc:/3ds/pocket-duty/packs/` and then `romfs:/packs/` (the map `bun tools/n3ds.ts build --embed <id>` put in the `.3dsx`). **A map's picture from above is the pack `<id>-map` of the same list** (`tools/packs.ts` `chartBytes`, `duty_interface::Chart`): a console uploads its image entry into the interface's core and tells the interface the texture (`chart` of the state); the browser's kernel tells it the entry as base64 and the interface uploads it. `tools/ui.ts` compiles no picture into the interface. **A change of map on the 3DS runs QuickJS's cycle collector once** (`guest_collect`, `n3ds/src/guest_qjs.c`): the screens the interface takes down leave about 59 KB of Solid owners a change that reference counts do not free; the log line `Map <id>: heap …` (`bun tools/n3ds.ts emu --log`) shows the C heap across each change. A map read again after a delivery (`bring`) leaves a person at the title there; `map=` and the interface's list begin a match.
- **The walk grid has a node a floor.** A cell of the grid (0.8 m) has one node, and a further node for each floor that stands over its first (`Level::over`, sorted by cell after the grid's `nx × nz`): a step goes to whichever node of the neighbouring cell is within a step's height, so a stair's head and a bridge's end join two floors with no record of their own. **Ask the grid for a node with a height** (`Level::node_at`, `nearest_open`), never by a cell alone, and walk it by node (`Level::toward`, `walkable`). A ladder is a link (`Level::links`: its foot, where a climber steps off at its top, the two nodes): the pathfinder takes it as a step that costs five times a walk, the match carries a soldier along it and on to the node by its far end (`Soldier::climb`), and the player hangs on it (`Player::hang`). The compiler leaves open only the largest part that hangs together across both sets and the ladders (`rules::nav`), so every way a soldier asks for exists. A place of one level has no further node and no ladder: its `NAVG` is the bytes it was before, and the pathfinder searches it with a loop compiled without either question (`Pathfinder::search::<false>`). **A search opens a node once.** Its estimate of the way left is the distance in the steps' own costs (`Pathfinder::estimate`: 6 eighths of a metre a straight step, 8 one across a corner), leaned to twice that (`LEAN`); a node's cost is settled when it is opened; a dearer heap entry of an opened node is passed over at an eighth of a node's budget; a search gives up after 16 000 nodes (`LIMIT`). The way is pulled straight in the steps after the search, five points a step of 120 nodes (`Pathfinder::pull`), and holds 32 points (`POINTS`): one with more corners ends at the last corner it holds, and its walker asks again from there. `cargo run --release -p duty-sim --example grid -- <pack>` prints the parts and the ladders; `--example match -- <pack> [minutes]` plays matches on the host and prints a tick's time and the score; `--example ways -- <pack>` times a way between every two starts.
- The frame budget is **33.3 ms** at 30 frames a second. The world is drawn in two depth passes: the cells whose square is within 12 m of the eye, then the rest (`near_square` in `psp/src/gfx.rs`). A soldier and an effect go in the pass of the cell they stand in. Do not choose a pass by a chunk's own box: a ray from the eye must meet every near cell before every far one.
- The GE drops a triangle with a vertex outside its 4096-pixel coordinate space. A cluster's large triangles come first in its run and the device tests them when the cluster's box is not inside the band (`duty_sim::clip`). The box lies along the level's axes (`Scene::level_x`, `level_z`), not the world's.
- A renderer or simulation change reports `bun tools/psp.ts bench` and `bun tools/psp.ts tour` from a console before and after (late frames, `cpuMs`, `listUs`, `simUs`, `geMs`), and `bun tools/psp.ts emu` for what the frame looks like. The emulator's times count instructions: they miss the floating point unit, the data cache and the bus, which are most of a console's frame, and they have ranked changes the wrong way round.
- On a console **a multiply and an add are 20 cycles**, **a line of the data cache fetched while the GE draws is a microsecond**, and **a word of `sceGu`'s list is a bus cycle** (`probe=1` in `boot.txt` writes `probe.json`). So: ask a chunk before its clusters and a cluster before its triangles; read the pack in order and keep what is asked again; write the world's GE commands through `gfx::Sub`, not `sceGu`. A new cost in the frame gets a timer in the status (`listUs`, `simUs`) before it gets an optimisation.
- The GE takes **0.7 ms for a thousand triangles** it is sent, and what the CPU reads from memory slows it. The frame has two budgets (`Gfx::budget` triangles, a soldier's counting for one and a half; `BUSY` milliseconds in `psp/src/main.rs`); over either the levels of detail move in. `cargo run --release -p duty-sim --example view -- <pack> x y z yaw pitch` and `bun tools/duty.ts shot` say what a view sends and what of it shows.
- A turn of the interface is **9 ms on a console** (5 ms of script, 4 ms of the core's list) whatever changed. A turn that would bring the guest only new readouts is taken every second frame, every third when frames are tight (`Pace::due`).
- The game in a browser tab is `wgpu/`: the match and the flow compiled to wasm32 and a wgpu renderer that reads the PSP's pack (`psp30`) as it is, inside PocketJS's Pocket3D player. The reference (`web/`) is never published as the game. A change to `psp/src/gfx.rs` or `psp/src/figures.rs` that changes the picture is made in `wgpu/src` in the same pull request, or named under "What differs from the PSP's picture" in `wgpu/README.md`. `wgpu/` holds no page chrome of its own: the bar, the shell, the controls and the dock (Download, Remix) are the kernel's, and the game's host on Pocket Studio may draw the dock itself.
- `wgpu/src/shaders/` gives the street's draw record and a figure's record a program file each: wgpu resolves a module's resources by group and binding, so two records on one binding take each other's size. A corner's packed numbers are bound as integers and scaled in the program.
- **A device the game has a package for is a device the browser page plays as, in the same change.** A new target in `tools/release.ts` comes with its shape in `wgpu/src/app.rs`, its interface bundle in `tools/wgpu.ts` (`SHOWN`), its entry in `DEVICES` and `runsOn` of `wgpu/page/main.js` and its name in `<meta name="pocket-devices">` of `wgpu/page/index.html`; `tools/wgpu.test.ts` fails when these lists differ, and `bun tools/wgpu.ts check` plays a match as each device. Deploy the site (`bun tools/wgpu.ts dist`, `pocket-studio site`) before uploading the package (`bun tools/release.ts --upload`).
- Resolve a rebase with `git add <file>` for the files that conflicted, never `git add -A`: the submodule's checkout is staged with everything else, and a checkout that lags the branch moves the pin back (it happened in #4).
- A change to `wgpu/` or to the page reports `bun tools/wgpu.ts check` (Chrome with WebGPU: the title, a match by keys, the menu, a fall, the results, the player in three windows) beside `cargo test --manifest-path wgpu/Cargo.toml`; a change to what a frame draws reports `bun tools/wgpu.ts shot` pictures before and after, and the frame's distance from `bun tools/duty.ts shot` of the same view.
- The interface is one PocketJS app, `ui/`, drawn over the street. **The device crate draws no text and no 2D.** `ui/app/protocol.ts` and `crates/duty-interface` are the two sides of one protocol: change both in one commit, with `cargo test -p duty-interface` and `bun tools/ui.ts test`.
- A number that changes during a match is a member of `t` written to its node through `@pocketjs/framework/hot`, never a signal. Look at every screen with `bun tools/ui.ts preview` before a device build.
- The Pocket3D title card plays first at every launch (`pocket3d_title::play` in `psp/src/main.rs`); a test run skips it with `title=0` in `boot.txt`. The icon is the Pocket3D icon from `vendor/pocketjs/engine/pocket3d/icon/` (`tools/psp.ts`, `psp/Psp.toml`). `psp/assets/pic1.png` is a capture of this game (`bun tools/psp.ts emu --no-interface --frames 24`).
- PSP: `bun tools/psp.ts run` resets PSPLINK and uses the share of whichever `usbhostfs_pc` is running. It takes PocketJS's `psp:usb` lease; do not pass `--take` over another session's run. After a reset, send nothing until `usbhostfs_pc` logs the new connection.
- PSP: the program needs the 52 MB of a PSP-2000 or later (`MEMSIZE` in the EBOOT's `PARAM.SFO`): the pack alone is 30 MB. Everything is built at `opt-level = 3`.
- Keep captures, logs, DutyIR, packs, USB-share contents and build receipts in `.pocket-build/`. Put reproducible commands, results and limits in the PR description instead of committing them.
- Separate evidence kinds when reporting: host build and tests, compile receipt, reference capture, emulator frame and status, device frame timing (`bench`), device capture, and what a person saw on the console.
- 3DS: `n3ds/` is a C host (`src/main.c`, the PICA200 renderer `src/render.c`, the PocketJS guest `src/guest.c`) linked with a Rust static library (`n3ds/core`, target `armv6k-nintendo-3ds`) that holds the match and hands the host plain records (`src/core.h`). `bun tools/n3ds.ts build` compiles both in PocketJS's pinned devkitARM container. The program is a `.3dsx` with the pack and the interface in its ROMFS, **at most 32 MiB** (the size the paired wire installs). Do not build a CIA.
- 3DS: the console is reached over PocketJS's paired LAN wire. The pairing key is a local file under `vendor/pocketjs/.pocket/3ds/devices/`; it is never committed. `bun tools/n3ds.ts install`, `status`, `ctl`, `capture` and `bench` use it; `emu` runs the same `.3dsx` in Azahar.
- 3DS: one depth pass over 24 bits, and the rasterizer clips, so `profiles/n3ds30.json` leaves out the large-triangle records (`"records": false`) and the core selects with `Guard::open()`. Ground is drawn once, with the shadow picture on the second texture unit; the triangle budget counts `Counts::once`.
- 3DS: a draw through `C3D_DrawElements` costs **16 microseconds on an Old 3DS** (citro3d looks at all 96 uniform registers each call). The street writes its uniforms, the buffer offset and the draw as GPU commands (`raw_uniform`, `raw_triangles` in `render.c`) and calls `C3Di_UpdateContext` only after a texture or combiner change: **7 microseconds a draw**. Time a renderer change with `partsUs` in the status and `bun tools/n3ds.ts bench` on an Old 3DS, not in Azahar.
- 3DS: sound goes to `ndsp` when `ndspInit` succeeds (it needs the DSP firmware at `sdmc:/3ds/dspfirm.cdc`) and to CSND otherwise (`"sound"` in the status says which).
- 3DS: the lower screen is the same PocketJS app's second surface (`ui/pocket.json`, `ui/app/presentations/dual.tsx`): the level from above and four keys. A key sends `{"type":"tap","key":…}`; every screen the PSP has keeps the PSP's layout on the upper screen.
- Vita: `vita/` is a Rust host on PocketJS's Vita host library (`hosts/vita`: vita2d, QuickJS, the UI core, the wired debug host) and `pocket-vita-gxm`, built by `cargo-vita` with the toolchain that library pins. It draws the browser renderer's frame (`wgpu/src/render.rs`) with GXM at **960 × 544, four samples a pixel, straight into the display's surface, 60 frames a second**. A change to that frame's order or to a program in `wgpu/src/shaders/` is made in `vita/src/world.rs`, `vita/src/figures.rs` and `vita/shaders/` in the same pull request.
- Vita: the console is debugged only through **Pocket Devkit** (PocketJS `apps/devkit`): `bun tools/vita.ts serve` is the USB host, `run` builds a SELF for Devkit's title and puts it in Devkit's native slot. Devkit's title is `P25BFE5E2`; a Devkit installed before PocketJS's rename is `P3B1D7273`, and the host's log names the title the console asks for (`pocket-vita/<title>/session.json`): pass `--title` or `DUTY_VITA_TITLE`. Keep the host running detached and one host to a cable. A push of the build that is running is refused: words for the start (`boot.txt`) take a new build. The package (`PKDT00001`) carries no debug link.
- Vita: the frame's two budgets are this thread's and the GPU's, and **the GPU's is the tight one**: 12.95 ms for 116 000 triangles of the street and two soldiers, against 8 ms of this thread. **A thousand triangles of the street are 0.043 ms of the GPU; four samples a pixel are 4.1 ms; the interface is 1.3 to 2.3 ms.** `BUDGET` in `vita/src/main.rs` is 170 000 triangles; over it, or when a frame waits 6 ms for the frame before last, the levels' distances come in. A renderer change reports `bun tools/vita.ts bench --clock 60` (late frames, `cpuMs`) and `bun tools/vita.ts cost` (the GPU's time for one held view, each part left out in turn) from a console before and after.
- Vita: **a draw is 3 microseconds of this thread**, so joining runs is worth little: two runs of a chunk are joined across 50 triangles (`Look::merge`); across 200 the GPU takes 0.48 ms more for the triangles between.
- Vita: a capture stops the frame it is taken in for 100 ms. Take captures outside a `bench`.
- Vita: the programs are Cg (`vita/shaders/`), compiled on the console by SceShaccCg at the first start and kept on the memory card under the hash of their source (`ux0:data/pocket-duty/gxp`); a development build copies them to the share with `manifest.txt`. `bun tools/vita.ts package` takes them from there: after a change to a program, run the development build on a console before packaging. `bun tools/vita.ts emu` stages the package for Vita3K, which needs those packaged programs (it has no shader compiler without a console's firmware); it has not run to a status yet: Vita3K stops at its own config when that names a VitaFS that is gone.
- Cook: **a cut-out's face is lit along its stored normal unless the profile says `light.leaves: "both"`**, and a leaf whose normal looks at the ground or away from the sun then bakes black (the black dots among the weeds). With the key a leaf takes the light of the side that has more of it. Every profile sets it; the change moves cut-out chunks' vertices and what follows from them (their clusters, large triangles and sight bits), nothing else.
- Cook: **the sight lists are kept under a hash that takes in the vertices' colours**, so a change of baked light runs the sight pass again (nine tenths of a cook). `DUTY_RAYS=20000` shortens it for a trial pack; a pack that ships is cooked with the profile's rays.
- Cook: `duty-cook shot` (`bun tools/duty.ts shot`) draws from the large triangles' records and stops on a pack without them (the 3DS's, `clip.records: false`). To look at the 3DS's pictures and light on this computer, cook with a copy of its profile that keeps the records: every other section is the same bytes.
- `bun tools/wgpu.ts shot --map <id> --pack FILE` draws the map's own cooked PSP pack and not FILE: `--pack` is the first map's. Give another map's pack as `--pack` alone.
- Vita: pictures are `texture::RGBA8` (`"format": "rgba"`), swizzled by the transfer engine up to 512 a side; the engine refuses 2 048 (`0x805b0003`), so a larger picture stays as rows. **The compiler never makes a picture larger than its source** (`Image::reduced` asserts it): a profile's `side` is the most a picture keeps.
- Vita: a development build keeps a map's pieces on the memory card (`ux0:data/pocket-duty/packs`) and fetches from the share the pieces the card lacks, so a cook sends only what changed. `host0:` serves a file from its start to its end: no seek and no size request (`vita/src/hostfs.rs`).
